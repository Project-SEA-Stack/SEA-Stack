ROV umbilical -- lazy-wave S-bend from a support-vessel stern A-frame to a seabed crawler
--
-- NOTE ON COMMENTS: MoorDyn parses each section until it hits a line containing
-- three dashes, and does NOT skip "--" comment lines inside a section body. All
-- commentary therefore lives here in the preamble; the sections below carry data
-- rows only. Do not put a "--" line between the section header and its data.
--
-- COUPLED BODIES
-- Both ends are Coupled bodies driven kinematically by SEA-Stack,
-- not by MoorDyn:
--    Body1 = support vessel  (Chrono body frame = its CoG, world z = -1.75 m)
--    Body2 = M113 crawler chassis on the seabed
-- The BODIES X0..y0 columns are nominal start-up poses only. MoorDynWrapper::
-- Initialize passes the actual Chrono body states to MoorDyn_Init, so the real
-- poses win. The crawler z in particular comes from the terrain height query
-- at runtime, not from this file.
--
-- POINT OFFSETS ARE MEASURED FROM EACH BODY'S CENTRE OF MASS
-- Points attached to BodyN are offsets in that body's own frame, and both Chrono
-- bodies here report their centre-of-mass frame, so the offsets are NOT measured
-- from a hull baseline or a chassis reference point.
--   Point 1 = vessel CoG + (26, 0, 6.75) = world (26, 0, +5): just aft of the
--             stern at x = +25 and above the deck at z = +3.5, i.e. at the head
--             of a stern A-frame.
--   Point 4 = crawler CoG + (-2, 0, 1.2): a towing eye 2 m aft and 1.2 m up.
--   Points 2 and 3 only join the three sections. Their coordinates are an initial
--             guess; MoorDyn's IC relaxation replaces them with the equilibrium.
--
-- LAZY-WAVE LAYOUT (three lines, buoyancy on the middle one)
--
--     fairlead (vessel stern, z = +5 m)
--        \                 _.-'''-._      hog bend, lifted by the buoyant section
--         \   upper 40 m _-'         '-_
--          '-._        _-'               '-._   lower 28 m
--              '--''''                       '-.__ crawler (z ~ -39.4 m)
--                                                 ~12 m of it lying on the bottom
--
-- Slant range fairlead to crawler at t = 0 is 49 m against 80 m of cable (1.6x),
-- so there is real slack. The arch is what decouples vessel heave from the
-- crawler: without it every heave cycle would snatch the vehicle. The
-- bottom-lying tail is what lets the crawler drive away without immediately
-- loading the cable.
--
-- BUOYANCY BUDGET
-- MoorDyn takes weight from Mass/m and buoyancy from Diam, so at rho = 1025:
--   Umbil    displaces  8.05 kg/m, weighs 12 kg/m -> sinks at  3.95 kg/m (38.8 N/m)
--   Buoyant  displaces 38.96 kg/m, weighs 25 kg/m -> lifts at 13.96 kg/m (137 N/m)
-- The 12 m buoyant section lifts about 1.64 kN against the 1.09 kN submerged
-- weight of the 28 m lower section it holds up. Buoyancy dominant by about
-- 0.55 kN, which gives a stable hog bend rather than a sagging line, and which
-- also relieves the fairlead: expect FairTen1 of order 1 to 2 kN in still water,
-- not the 3.1 kN the whole 80 m would weigh if it all hung from the ship.
--
-- AXIAL STIFFNESS, SEGMENT LENGTH AND dtM ARE ONE CHOICE, NOT THREE
-- MoorDyn integrates the lines explicitly (RK2), so dtM has to resolve the fastest
-- axial segment mode. Per segment k = EA/l and m = (Mass/m)*l, so w = sqrt(k/m),
-- and MoorDyn's rule of thumb is dtM < 0.1/w. With the numbers below:
--   Umbil,   l = 2.5 m: k = 24 MN/m, m = 30.0 kg -> w =  894 rad/s -> dtM < 1.1e-4
--   Buoyant, l = 1.5 m: k = 40 MN/m, m = 37.5 kg -> w = 1033 rad/s -> dtM < 9.7e-5
-- dtM = 5e-5 s therefore has about a factor of two in hand, and means 10 MoorDyn
-- substeps per 5e-4 s Chrono coupling step. For reference, dtM = 5e-4 s diverges
-- to NaN in the buoyant section within 25 steps.
--
-- EA = 60 MN is the SOFT end of the realistic 50 to 200 MN range for a 100 mm
-- umbilical, i.e. a lightly armoured cable rather than a heavily armoured power
-- umbilical. It is chosen deliberately: a stiffer cable would need a dtM small
-- enough that MoorDyn's initial-condition relaxation (TmaxIC below) dominates the
-- run time, and axial stiffness has almost no bearing on what this demo shows,
-- which is the geometry and heave-decoupling of a lazy wave. Raise EA if you care
-- about snap loads, and drop dtM with it.
--
-- SEABED
-- WtrDpth must match the BEM solve and the demo sea state; the demo asserts it.
-- MoorDyn's seabed here is FLAT at the height-map crest level (z = -40 m), so the
-- cable rests on the high spots and bridges the hollows. The 1.5 m of bathymetry
-- is felt only by the crawler's tracks.
